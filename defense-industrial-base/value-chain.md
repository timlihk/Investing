# Defense Industrial Base — Value Chain & Chokepoint Map

*Market data as of 2026-08-03 (Bloomberg close). Capacity, contract and policy figures are sourced — see [Sources](#sources). Chokepoint scores, tiers and verdicts are analyst judgement.*

## Core Thesis

The US is running the largest munitions capacity expansion since the Cold War. Lockheed alone is quadrupling THAAD and PrSM capacity and has tripled PAC-3 MSE. The FY2027 request puts roughly **$95bn** into missiles and munitions — about a tripling year-over-year.

The investment question is **not** "who wins the contracts." It is:

> **Where does the chain physically narrow, and who gets paid at that point?**

Three findings drive everything in this book.

### 1. The expansion headlines are capex, not revenue — for the announcer

Lockheed's ">$9bn" facility programme is money Lockheed **spends**. The market has already priced this asymmetry:

| Bearing the capex | YTD | | Collecting the capex | YTD |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| L3Harris | **−5.6%** | | Ducommun | **+88.2%** |
| Northrop | **−4.9%** | | AAR | +69.2% |
| Boeing | −0.5% | | Carpenter Technology | +65.1% |
| Huntington Ingalls | −4.0% | | ATI | +63.3% |
| Lockheed | +20.5% | | Moog | +60.1% |

*(S&P 500 +9.4%, ITA +11.6% over the same period.)*

L3Harris **beat and raised** on 29 July 2026 and fell ~7% — missile-segment capex ran **+92% YTD** and the missile-business IPO slipped. Northrop beat with a **record $105bn backlog** and fell on capital intensity plus B-21 margin drag.

**Buying the prime on the announcement is the worst expression of this trade.**

### 2. Money is not the binding constraint

After **$6bn** invested, 155mm output was still **~56k shells/month against a 100k goal** as of February 2026. The stated blockers are energetics, tooling and trained personnel — none of which respond to a budget line within a fiscal year. This is why the map below is ordered by **replaceability**, not by revenue.

### 3. The international complex has decoupled from the US bottleneck

Europe and Korea de-rated hard in 2026 — but on **war-consumption** risk, not supply structure:

- **Ukraine/Middle East peace expectations** across the whole European complex
- **Germany reportedly scrapped the F126 frigate programme**, where Rheinmetall was to be lead contractor — the stock fell 18% on the news, proving procurement remains political
- **Korean Q2 earnings shock** — Hanwha Aerospace OP ₩947.3bn vs ₩1.02tn consensus; Korean defence names fell 31–64% from peaks
- **Middle East contract delays** on Gulf fiscal strain

The US thesis is a **stockpile-rebuild and industrial-capacity** story that survives a ceasefire. Japanese input supply survives it too. European ammunition volumes and Korean Gulf exports largely do not. **These are being priced as one trade. They are not one trade.**

## Where the Chokepoints Actually Are

| Layer | Key names | Assessment | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ammonium perchlorate** | NEU (AMPAC) | **25/25 — Monopoly** | Sole large-scale US producer. No AP, no solid rockets. $100m expansion, >50% capacity increase at Cedar City UT |
| **Titanium sponge** | 5726 JP, 5727 JP | **23/25 — Monopoly** | US has zero domestic capacity; Japan = 73% of US imports 2025; VSMPO (Russian) unavailable |
| **Solid rocket motors** | NOC, LHX | **22/25 — Duopoly** | Six→two over 30 years. `alternatives=4` makes the duopoly explicit; qualification and capacity both score 5, which is why it behaves like a monopoly |
| **Rare-earth magnets** | MP, USAR | **22/25 — Monopoly** | 2027 China-origin ban is a legislated demand mandate, not a forecast |
| **Ablatives / motor cases** | PKE | **21/25 — Monopoly** | Textbook cheap-but-critical: `cogs=5`. Tiny $ content holding up an entire missile |
| **Aerospace carbon fibre** | HXL, 3402 JP | 19/25 — Duopoly | Aerospace qualification takes years |
| **Premium alloys / forgings** | ATI, CRS, 5631 JP | 19/25 — Duopoly | Not generic metal — qualified premium alloy and large-forging capacity |
| **European energetics** | CHG LN, RHM GY | 20 / 17 — Duopoly | Europe outputs ~4,500–10,000 t/yr propellant vs >6,000 t/yr for Ukraine alone and ~20,000 t stockpile targets |
| **RF / seekers** | MTSI, BA (seekers) | 19 / 16 | Relieving motors exposes this layer immediately |
| **Tracked transmissions** | R3NK GY | 18/25 — Duopoly | Genuinely narrow European land-systems layer |
| **Platform assembly** | 064350 KS, 047810 KS | 12–13 — Oligopoly | The **widest** part of the chain. Cheap for a reason |

## The Recursive Bottleneck

Every relieved constraint exposes the next one down. The cleanest illustration:

> Japan license-produces PAC-3 at **~30/yr** and completed the **first-ever export of Japanese-built PAC-3 to the US on 20 Nov 2025** (~10 units, per Nikkei) to refill American stockpiles. Tokyo and Washington see scope to **double output to ~60/yr** — *once Boeing-built seekers stop being the bottleneck.*

So the missile bottleneck was never the missile. It was the motor; then the oxidiser; and on the allied side, the seeker. **This is why the map is ordered by replaceability.**

## Key Research Questions

1. **Does the capex de-rating in NOC/LHX resolve, and when?** The capex is what creates 2028+ earnings, but it can compress FCF for another year. This is a 2–3 year position, not a quarter.
2. **How much of NEU's value is actually AMPAC?** The chokepoint is 25/25 but sits inside a petroleum-additives company with ~170 AMPAC employees. Quantify the segment before sizing.
3. **Does Golden Dome's $17.5bn survive reconciliation?** Only $400m is in the base request. Failure defers a large slice of the demand narrative.
4. **Do the SRM new entrants scale?** Anduril, X-Bow, Ursa Major and Mach Energetics all have DoD money. If they qualify at volume, the NOC/LHX duopoly premium erodes — the main structural risk to the highest-conviction US idea.
5. **Is the Korean de-rating earnings or thesis?** Q2 was an earnings miss and a Gulf-order delay, not a demand reversal. Q3 is the test.
6. **Verify the unverified.** IHI Aerospace's SRM position, and the defence-revenue split at Toray, Shin-Etsu and TDK, are industry knowledge in this build, **not** source-confirmed. Check segment disclosure before sizing any of them.

## Kill Conditions

- **A Ukraine and/or Middle East settlement.** Already the dominant driver of European and Korean prices. Rheinmetall and Poongsan take the war-continuation side; Hanwha's US plants and Japanese titanium do not.
- **Golden Dome fails reconciliation** — $17.5bn becomes $400m.
- **Continuing resolution / budget delay** pushes the whole FY27 munitions ramp right.
- **The capex cycle extends.** If LHX/NOC keep guiding capex up, cheap stays cheap.
- **SRM new entrants qualify at volume**, eroding the duopoly.
- **FX.** JPY/KRW/EUR assets against a USD-funded thesis; unhedged, currency can eat the edge.
