## Why this is the best risk/reward in the book

A Korean company is building **American energetics capacity** — the single hardest-to-relieve layer in the Western munitions chain — and even after the 4 Aug rally it trades at 17.2x forward with a +71% consensus gap.

| Investment | Detail | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Pine Bluff Arsenal, Arkansas | ~$1.3bn ammunition plant | announced |
| US modular charge facility | ~$1bn; **nitrocellulose, nitroglycerin, nitroguanidine, triple-base propellant** + load-assemble-pack line for 155mm modular charges | construction from 2026, full production 2030, ~$500m annual revenue, ~200 jobs |

That second line item is the thesis. The US 155mm programme has absorbed **$6bn** and still runs at **~56k shells/month against a 100k goal** because propellant chemistry, not assembly, is the constraint. Hanwha is building exactly that chemistry on US soil.

## Why it is cheap

- **Q2 2026 operating profit ₩947.3bn vs ₩1.02tn consensus** — a ~7% miss
- **Middle East contract delays** on Gulf fiscal strain
- Sector-wide de-rating: Korean defence fell **31–64% from peaks**; Hanwha Aerospace itself −43% from its March 2026 high

None of these are supply-structure events. They are an earnings miss and an order-timing slip inside a peace-headline de-rating.

## The decoupling argument

Korean defence sold off on **war-consumption** risk. Hanwha's US plants are funded by **US stockpile rebuild**, which is what happens *after* a war ends, not during one. This is the position in the book with the least peace-deal beta relative to its drawdown.

## Risks

- **Q3 is the test.** The de-rating was earnings-driven. Another miss and cheap gets cheaper.
- **Execution on US construction** — 2030 full production is a long runway with cost-overrun risk on unfamiliar regulatory ground.
- **KRW** against a USD-funded thesis, unhedged.
- **Gulf orders may not simply be delayed.** Fiscal strain can become cancellation.

## Kill condition

A Q3 miss *combined with* a formal cancellation (not deferral) of a major Middle East programme would break the earnings leg while the US capacity leg is still years from revenue.
