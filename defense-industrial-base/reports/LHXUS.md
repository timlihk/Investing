$277.86 · −5.4% YTD · 22.1x fwd · target $358.77 (**+29%**) · rec 4.32
*Market data: Bloomberg, 2026-08-04. Consensus target eased from $361.71 to $358.77.*

## The core position

L3Harris owns **Aerojet Rocketdyne** — one of only **two** domestic solid rocket motor primes. Industry consolidation took the US from six SRM makers to two over roughly thirty years, and CSIS and the Pentagon both name SRMs as a binding constraint on missile output.

`breakage 5 · alternatives 4 · qualification 5 · cogs 3 · capacity 5`

Note `alternatives = 4`, not 5 — this is a duopoly and the raw input says so. It lands in the monopoly tier because `qualification` and `capacity` are both maxed: two suppliers each running at their capacity ceiling behave, to a customer placing an order, like one.

## Why it is cheap — and why that is the thesis

On **29 July 2026** L3Harris **beat and raised** — revenue $5.9bn, EPS $3.13 (an ~11.8% beat), guidance up across key metrics — and the stock fell ~2.5% in session and a further ~4.4% after hours. It has since drifted to ~$278, and unlike most of the sector it did **not** participate in the 4 Aug risk-on move.

The reasons investors gave:
1. **Missile-business capex +65% QoQ and +92% YTD**
2. **The missile-business IPO/separation slipped**

Both are the thesis, not counter-evidence. The capex compressing today's FCF is precisely what builds the capacity that gets sold in 2028+. Meanwhile deliveries are up **>60%** since the Aerojet acquisition three years ago, with operational efficiency improved 22%.

**Expansion under way:** >$1bn at Orange County, Virginia (more than doubling manufacturing space, 350+ jobs over five years) plus $25m and +130k sq ft at Advanced Manufacturing Facility-South in Huntsville.

## Why LHX over NOC

Same duopoly, same chokepoint score. NOC is cheaper (18.8x vs 22.1x) but carries **~$2.0bn of cumulative B-21 LRIP losses with $1.0bn of accrual remaining** — fixed-price risk entirely unrelated to the missile thesis. LHX is the cleaner expression; NOC is the cheaper one. This book holds both.

## Catalyst

The **missile-business separation** is a dated, identifiable event that re-rates the SRM asset away from the conglomerate multiple. It has already slipped once, which is why it is priced as a maybe.

## Risks

- **The capex de-rating can persist.** If guidance keeps stepping up, this stays cheap through 2027. Underwrite 2–3 years, not two quarters.
- **New entrants.** Anduril, X-Bow, Ursa Major and Mach Energetics (which bought Exquadrum for $50m) all have DoD funding to break the duopoly. This is the main structural risk.
- **IPO slips again** and the catalyst evaporates.
- **Upstream constraint.** L3Harris cannot cast motors without ammonium perchlorate — see `NEUUS.md`. Relieving its own bottleneck exposes the one above it.
